THE COOPER CASE
Lubbock, Texas, 2004 — A Family Silenced

She Said His Name Was Butch

"How did you find me?" Those were among the last words Tammy Cooper spoke into the phone before the line went dead. On the other end, her friend heard a deep voice demanding Tammy get off the phone. Then silence. By morning, a mother and her three children would be found brutally beaten and stabbed to death in their northeast Lubbock apartment — and the man Tammy called "Butch" would vanish into the Texas night, leaving behind one of the state\'s most brutal unsolved murders.

It began with a phone call. On the evening of October 24, 2004, Tammy Cooper was talking with a close friend, catching up on the day\'s events, laughing about the small joys of life. Then came the knock at the door. One of the children — one of the twin boys — opened it. A man stepped inside. Tammy\'s friend heard everything through the receiver.

"Who is that?" the friend asked. "You don\'t know him," Tammy replied. "He\'s not from here. He\'s a black man, and his name is Butch." The tone was calm — too calm. If Tammy feared for her life, she gave no sign. The friend heard Butch\'s deep voice, agitated, demanding that Tammy end the call. They needed to talk. Then, without warning, the line went dead. The friend tried calling back. Nothing. Just the hollow ring of a phone that would never be answered again.

The next morning, unable to reach Tammy, the friend drove to the Windcrest Estacado apartments at 512 North Martin Luther King Jr. Boulevard. She entered the apartment and discovered a scene that would haunt her forever. Tammy Cooper, 45, lay dead. Her daughter Mahogany Jasmine Allen, 11, was dead. Her twin sons KaDiece and KaSheim Allen, both 9, were dead. All four had been brutally beaten and stabbed. Blood stained the walls. Furniture was overturned. The apartment bore the scars of a violent, prolonged struggle — a mother fighting for her life and her children\'s lives against a killer who would not stop until every breath was extinguished.

Former Lubbock County District Attorney Matt Powell, assigned to the case as a prosecutor, described the scene as unlike anything he had ever encountered. "You could tell that a significant struggle had taken place," Powell said. "There were a lot of things that had been disrupted within the kitchen area, within the living room area, and in some other areas in the house. She fought very valiantly for her life and for her kids\' lives." Senior Homicide Investigator Rey Martinez, who led the investigation, put it more bluntly: "It was like walking through a nightmare."

Twenty years later, the nightmare continues. The Lubbock Police Department has traveled to Dallas more than 20 times and to California chasing leads. A reward of $20,000 was offered. The name "Butch" — a nickname, an alias, perhaps a real first name — remains the only lead. No DNA match. No suspect identified. No arrest made. Just four graves in Lubbock and a question that haunts a city: Who is Butch? And where is he now?

THE QUESTION THAT HAUNTS LUBBOCK:
What kind of rage drives a man to murder a mother and her three children — to beat and stab an 11-year-old girl and two 9-year-old twin boys? How did Butch find Tammy after she moved from Dallas seeking a fresh start? And why — despite a known suspect name, a brutal crime scene with "plenty of evidence," and two decades of investigation — has no one been brought to justice for the murders of Tammy Cooper, Mahogany Jasmine Allen, KaDiece Allen, and KaSheim Allen?

Key Evidence Categories

Crime Scene

The Apartment

The Windcrest Estacado apartment at 512 N. MLK Blvd showed signs of extreme violence. Blood stained the walls. Furniture was overturned. The victims were found in different rooms — a scene described by investigators as "like walking through a nightmare."

Victim

Tammy Cooper — The Mother

Tammy Anita Cooper, 45, had moved her three children from Dallas to Lubbock just six months earlier, seeking a fresh start. On the night of the murders, she told a friend a man named "Butch" was at her door. Her last words: "How did you find me?"

Victim

Mahogany Jasmine Allen — Age 11

Mahogany Jasmine Allen was Tammy\'s 11-year-old daughter. She was found dead in the apartment, beaten and stabbed alongside her brothers. The violence against this child was described as extraordinarily brutal.

Victims

KaDiece & KaSheim Allen — The Twins, Age 9

KaDiece and KaSheim Allen were 9-year-old twin boys — full of energy, promise, and childhood innocence. They were found in another room of the apartment, both beaten and stabbed to death.

Suspect

"Butch" — The Unknown

The name "Butch" is the ghost at the center of this case. Tammy named him specifically in her final phone call. Police believe he was the last visitor to enter the Cooper home alive. He may be in the Dallas/Fort Worth area.

Investigation

The LPD Investigation

The Lubbock Police Department has pursued this case with extraordinary dedication. Detectives have traveled to California and Dallas over 20 times. In 2010, police announced a possible suspect in the Dallas/Fort Worth area. A $20,000 reward was offered.

Case Timeline

Every known movement and event from the night of the murders to the present investigation.

October 24, 2004 — Evening
The Final Phone Call

Tammy Cooper is on the phone with a close friend. During the conversation, she mentions a man at her door — someone she calls "Butch." The tone of the conversation shifts. The call is abruptly disconnected. The friend tries calling back but gets no answer. This is the last known contact with Tammy Cooper alive.

October 24, 2004 — Late Night
The Discovery

Unable to reach Tammy, the friend drives to the Windcrest Estacado apartments at 512 North Martin Luther King Jr. Boulevard in northeast Lubbock. She enters the apartment and discovers a scene of unimaginable horror. Tammy Cooper is dead. Her three children — Mahogany Jasmine, 11, and twins KaDiece and KaSheim, 9 — are also dead. All four have been brutally beaten and stabbed.

October 25, 2004
LPD Responds

The Lubbock Police Department responds to the crime scene. Senior Homicide Investigator Rey Martinez is assigned to lead the investigation. The scene is documented — blood on the walls, overturned furniture, signs of a violent struggle throughout the apartment. Autopsies are ordered. The case is immediately classified as a quadruple homicide.

October 2004 — November 2004
The Initial Investigation

Police launch an intensive investigation. The name "Butch" becomes the central focus. Detectives interview friends, family, and neighbors. The family had recently moved from Dallas, so investigators coordinate with Dallas police. A reward is posted for information leading to an arrest. Flyers are distributed throughout Lubbock and Dallas.

2004 — 2010
Years of Dead Ends

The investigation spans multiple states. Detectives travel to California and to Dallas more than 20 times following leads. Each promising tip seems to hit a wall. The identity of "Butch" remains elusive. The case grows cold but is never closed. The Lubbock Police Department continues to revisit evidence and re-interview witnesses.

February 10, 2010
New Information Released

Lubbock Police hold a news conference announcing new information in the case. Lead detective Rey Martinez reveals details about a possible suspect — information suggesting the suspect may be in the Dallas/Fort Worth Metroplex area. The reward is increased to $20,000. Police express confidence that the case can be solved with the right tip.

October 25, 2010
Six Years — Still Unsolved

On the six-year anniversary of the murders, media coverage renews public interest. KCBD NewsChannel 11 reports that the case remains one of Lubbock\'s most brutal unsolved crimes. The family continues to plead for answers. The apartment complex has been renamed Madison Park, but the memory of what happened in unit 512 lingers.

October 25, 2022
18th Anniversary

KCBD marks the 18th anniversary of the "Quad Murder." The case is still active but remains unsolved. The Lubbock Police Department continues to investigate. Family members speak out, keeping the victims\' memories alive. The name "Butch" is still the only lead — a ghost that haunts every anniversary.

October 24, 2024
20 Years — The Daughter Speaks

On the 20th anniversary of the murders, Tammy Cooper\'s daughter from a previous relationship opens up to the media. She speaks about the pain of two decades without justice, the memories of her mother and siblings, and her hope that someone will finally come forward with the truth. The case remains Lubbock\'s most infamous cold case.

Present Day
Active Cold Case

The Lubbock Police Department still lists the Cooper family murder as an active investigation. Detectives continue to follow up on tips. The $20,000 reward remains available. Crime Stoppers accepts anonymous tips at 806-741-1000. The question remains: Who is Butch? And where is he now?

The Evidence

Forensic findings, crime scene analysis, and the physical artifacts that define the Cooper family murder case.

Crime Scene

The Apartment

The Windcrest Estacado apartment at 512 N. MLK Blvd showed signs of extreme violence. Blood stained the walls. Furniture was overturned throughout the residence. The victims were found in different rooms — Tammy and Mahogany Jasmine in one area, the twin boys in another. The brutality suggested a personal, rage-fueled attack rather than a random burglary.

Forensic

Cause of Death

Autopsy results confirmed all four victims died from a combination of blunt force trauma and the use of a sharp instrument. The violence was described by investigators as extraordinarily brutal, particularly against the children. The murder weapon was never definitively identified or recovered.

Key Clue

"Butch" — The Name

The single most important clue in the case. Tammy Cooper was on the phone with a friend when she said "Butch" was at the door. The call disconnected immediately after. Police believe "Butch" was the killer. Despite extensive investigation, no positive identification has been made. The name may be a nickname, an alias, or a real first name.

Investigation

Multi-State Investigation

The Lubbock Police Department has traveled to California and Dallas over 20 times following leads. The family had recently relocated from Dallas, creating a complex web of potential suspects and connections. In 2010, police announced information suggesting the suspect may be in the Dallas/Fort Worth Metroplex area.

Theories & Suspects

Twenty years of investigation have produced multiple theories. These are the most compelling explanations for what happened to the Cooper family.

⚡
"Butch" — The Personal Connection
Probability: HIGH (per LPD)
  • Tammy Cooper named him specifically in her final phone call
  • The violence suggests a personal, rage-driven attack
  • Police believe he was the last visitor to the apartment
  • May have had a romantic or financial connection to Tammy
  • Possibly located in the Dallas/Fort Worth Metroplex
  • The name "Butch" may be a nickname or alias
🔍
Dallas Connection Theory
Probability: MODERATE
  • Family had recently moved from Dallas to Lubbock
  • Police traveled to Dallas over 20 times following leads
  • Suspect may have followed family from Dallas
  • Possible unresolved conflict from previous location
  • Tammy may have been escaping a dangerous situation
  • Fresh start was cut short by past catching up
🌐
The California Lead
Probability: UNKNOWN
  • Investigators traveled to California during early investigation
  • Unknown connection between family and West Coast
  • May relate to "Butch" having California ties
  • Lead never publicly explained in detail
  • Could indicate suspect\'s attempt to flee far from Texas
  • One of many dead ends in a sprawling investigation
⚠️
The Domestic Violence Theory
Probability: SPECULATIVE
  • Extreme violence against entire family including children
  • Suggests intimate partner violence or familial rage
  • "Butch" may have been a current or former partner
  • The attack was not a quick killing — it was annihilation
  • Children were targeted, not collateral damage
  • Killer wanted to erase the entire family unit
❓
The Random Violence Theory
Probability: LOW
  • Tammy named the killer specifically — not random
  • The brutality suggests personal motivation
  • No signs of forced entry or robbery as primary motive
  • Apartment was not ransacked for valuables
  • Random killers rarely target entire families this savagely
  • However: some serial killers do target families
🕯️
The Cover-Up Theory
Probability: UNKNOWN
  • Twenty years with no arrest despite "plenty of evidence"
  • Police know more than they have publicly revealed
  • "Butch" may be protected by family or community
  • Witness intimidation may have silenced key information
  • The case may be solvable but requires one brave witness
  • Someone knows who Butch is — and has stayed silent
