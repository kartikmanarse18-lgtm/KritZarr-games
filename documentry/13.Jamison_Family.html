JAMISON FAMILY
Eufaula, Oklahoma, 2009 — A True Crime Investigation

The Zombie Walk

They moved like sleepwalkers through their own driveway—back and forth, back and forth, twenty times in silence, loading their truck with methodical precision yet carrying nothing at all. On October 8, 2009, Bobby Jamison, his wife Sherilynn, and their six-year-old daughter Madyson drove into the Sans Bois Mountains to look at a piece of land. They were never seen alive again. Four years later, hunters found their skeletal remains lying face-down, side by side, less than three miles from their abandoned truck. The cause of death: undetermined. The $32,000 in cash left behind: untouched. The questions: endless.

It began with ghosts. In the months before their disappearance, the Jamison family was unraveling. Bobby, a 44-year-old man with chronic back pain from a 2003 car accident, and Sherilynn, a 40-year-old woman battling bipolar disorder, had become convinced their Eufaula lake house was haunted. Their daughter Madyson spoke to an imaginary friend named Emily—whom Sherilynn believed was a demonic entity. Bobby asked their pastor if he knew where to buy "special bullets" to shoot the spirits he claimed were living on their roof. He purchased a copy of The Satanic Bible, hoping to perform his own exorcism. Sherilynn conducted seances and wrote ominous messages on their storage container: "3 cats killed to date by people in this area... Witches don't like their black cats killed."

The family's surveillance camera captured their final hours in what investigators would later call "the zombie walk." Bobby and Sherilynn moved in a trance-like state, walking silently between their house and truck, sometimes carrying items, sometimes carrying nothing, their faces vacant and pale. They had lost significant weight. They looked, as one investigator noted, like people who had already left their bodies behind. The footage timestamped October 8, 2009, would become the last known images of them alive.

They drove thirty miles to Red Oak in the Sans Bois Mountains to inspect a forty-acre plot of land they planned to buy. They had already purchased a large storage container to live in. Their truck was found eight days later, locked, with $32,000 in cash hidden beneath the driver's seat, their wallets, IDs, phones, GPS, and their malnourished dog Maisie still inside. There was no sign of struggle. No blood. No broken glass. Just an empty truck in the middle of a dirt road, as if the family had stepped out and vanished into the Oklahoma wilderness.

On November 16, 2013, deer hunters stumbled upon three skulls in the Smokestack Hollow area of Panola Mountain. The remains were confirmed as Bobby, Sherilynn, and Madyson—lying face-down, side by side, their bodies so decomposed that no cause of death could ever be determined. One skull had a small hole that might have been a gunshot wound, but the theory was abandoned. The case remains open, the theories multiplying like the mountain shadows: murder by Bobby's estranged father, a drug deal gone wrong, a satanic cult hit list, mass suicide, or something even darker that walked them into those woods and never let them leave.

THE QUESTION THAT HAUNTS EVERYONE:
What force—human or otherwise—could make three people walk into the wilderness and die together, face-down in the dirt, leaving behind a fortune in cash, their vehicle, and their faithful dog? The Jamison family disappearance remains one of America's most chilling unsolved mysteries, a case where every theory seems possible and none can be proven.

Key Evidence Categories

Critical Evidence

The Zombie Walk Footage

The family's surveillance camera captured Bobby and Sherilynn making twenty silent trips between their house and truck in a trance-like state. They moved with vacant expressions, sometimes carrying nothing at all. The footage has been analyzed by experts who describe their movements as drug-like or dissociative.

Crime Scene

The Abandoned Truck

The Jamisons' locked pickup was discovered on a remote dirt road in Latimer County with $32,000 in cash under the driver's seat, their wallets, IDs, cell phones, GPS unit, and their malnourished dog Maisie. The vehicle was in working order with fuel. No signs of struggle were found.

Primary Victim

Bobby Jamison — The Father

A 44-year-old man with chronic back pain from a 2003 car accident. He was suing his father Bob Dean Jamison at the time of disappearance, claiming his father threatened to kill the family. Bobby believed their home was haunted and asked his pastor about "special bullets" to shoot spirits.

Primary Victim

Sherilynn Jamison — The Mother

A 40-year-old woman suffering from bipolar disorder who had attempted suicide in September 2009. She conducted seances, believed in witchcraft, and wrote threatening messages about their cats being killed. She owned a .22 caliber pistol that was never recovered. An 11-page "hate letter" to Bobby was found in the truck.

Child Victim

Madyson Jamison — The Daughter

Six years old. The last known photo of her was found on Bobby's phone, taken in the mountains. She appears unhappy with arms crossed, leading some to believe someone other than her parents took the picture. She had been pulled out of school before the disappearance.

Location

The Sans Bois Mountains

A remote, densely wooded mountain range in southeastern Oklahoma known for drug activity and isolation. The Jamisons' remains were found 2.7 miles from their truck in an area with no paved roads for miles. The terrain is rugged, with oak-pine-hickory forest covering steep slopes.

Case Timeline

Every documented event from the family's psychological unraveling to the discovery of their remains and the theories that followed.

2003
The Car Accident

Bobby Jamison is involved in a serious car accident that leaves him with chronic back pain. This event marks the beginning of the family's physical and psychological decline. The injuries would later make it unlikely that Bobby could have hiked deep into the mountains on foot.

November 2008
The Father's Threat

Bobby's father, Bob Dean Jamison, allegedly hits Bobby with his vehicle and threatens to kill the entire family. Bobby files a protective order describing his father as a "very dangerous man who thinks he is above the law" and involved in "prostitutes, gangs, and meth." The order is dismissed in May 2009.

July 2009
Custody Battle & Suicide Attempt

Sherilynn's ex-husband gains custody of their son Colton. During the hearing, 12-year-old Colton testifies that his mother seemed "very depressed" and "acted strangely." In September 2009, Sherilynn is hospitalized after a failed suicide attempt. The family's mental health crisis deepens.

August 2009
The Handyman Incident

Family friend Kenneth Bellows, a handyman with white supremacist leanings, is forced to leave the Jamison home at gunpoint after Sherilynn discovers his racist views and her Native American heritage becomes an issue. Sherilynn fires a .22 caliber pistol into the ground by his feet and points it at him.

September 2009
Spiritual Warfare

The family tells their pastor, Gary Brandon, that they are experiencing "spiritual warfare." Bobby asks about purchasing "special bullets" to shoot spirits on their roof. Sherilynn believes their daughter Madyson's imaginary friend Emily is a demonic entity. Bobby buys The Satanic Bible to perform an exorcism.

October 8, 2009
The Zombie Walk & Disappearance

Surveillance footage captures Bobby and Sherilynn walking back and forth between their house and truck in a trance-like state, making approximately twenty trips. They appear gaunt and vacant. The family then drives to Red Oak in the Sans Bois Mountains to inspect a 40-acre property. They are never seen alive again.

October 16, 2009
The Truck Is Found

Hunters on dirt bikes discover the Jamisons' abandoned, locked truck on a remote dirt road in Latimer County. Inside: $32,000 in cash, wallets, IDs, cell phones, GPS unit, maps, and the family's malnourished dog Maisie. An 11-page "hate letter" from Sherilynn to Bobby is also found. A 300-person search finds nothing.

November 12, 2009
The Mysterious Phone Call

Phone records show an outgoing call to voicemail was made from one of the cell phones left inside the locked, abandoned truck. This impossible detail deepens the mystery—who made the call, and how? The phone had been locked inside the vehicle since October 8.

December 2009
Bobby's Father Dies

Bob Dean Jamison, Bobby's estranged father and primary suspect, dies of natural causes at age 64. He had been in a hospital or rest home at the time of the disappearance, giving him a solid alibi. The FBI had cleared him as a person of interest, but questions about his possible involvement through associates remain.

November 16, 2013
The Remains Are Discovered

Two deer hunters in the Smokestack Hollow area of Panola Mountain discover three human skulls and skeletal remains. The location is 2.7 miles from where the truck was found, in extremely remote terrain. The bodies are lying face-down, side by side. Shoes, clothing fragments, and bone fragments are scattered nearby.

July 3, 2014
Official Identification

After eight months of anthropological and forensic pathological testing, the Oklahoma Medical Examiner's Office officially confirms the remains belong to Bobby, Sherilynn, and Madyson Jamison. Due to extensive decomposition and animal predation, no cause of death can be determined. The case remains officially unsolved.

Present Day
The Mystery Endures

Multiple theories continue to circulate: murder by Bobby's father or his associates, a drug deal gone wrong, involvement with a satanic cult, mass suicide, or something supernatural. The $32,000 in cash, the zombie walk footage, the locked truck, and the face-down positioning of the bodies remain unexplained. No one has ever been charged.

The Evidence

Physical findings, documented testimonies, and the inexplicable details that define the Jamison family mystery.

Critical Evidence

The Zombie Walk Surveillance

Home security footage shows Bobby and Sherilynn making approximately twenty silent trips between their house and truck on the morning of October 8, 2009. They move with vacant expressions, methodical precision, and trance-like dissociation. Sometimes they carry items; sometimes they carry nothing. Their gaunt appearance and strange behavior have led experts to suggest drug influence, severe depression, or something more sinister.

Crime Scene

$32,000 in Untouched Cash

Inside the locked truck, investigators found $32,000 in cash stuffed in a bank bag beneath the driver's seat. The family was on disability benefits at the time. The money was untouched, ruling out simple robbery. Theories about the cash include: funds for the land purchase, drug money, or savings from years of work at Bobby's father's gas station.

Behavioral Evidence

Spiritual Warfare & The Satanic Bible

The family told their pastor they were engaged in "spiritual warfare" with entities in their home. Bobby asked about "special bullets" to shoot spirits. Sherilynn conducted seances and wrote messages about witches and poisoned cats on their storage container. A copy of The Satanic Bible and a "witch's bible" were found in their home after the disappearance.

Forensic Evidence

The Face-Down Positioning

The three bodies were found lying face-down, side by side, less than three miles from their truck. This positioning suggests execution-style placement rather than natural death from exposure or getting lost. One skull had a small hole initially suspected to be from a gunshot, though this theory was later abandoned by investigators.
