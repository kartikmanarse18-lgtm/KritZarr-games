GHOST CHILD
Frederick, Colorado, 2018 — A Bodycam Mystery

The Ghost In The Frame

"You can clearly see a little girl," Arthur Cant wrote beneath his YouTube video. In the police bodycam footage from the Watts family home, as officers searched the rooms and questioned a visibly nervous Chris Watts, a small figure moved in the background of an upstairs bedroom. The light turned off by itself. A child appeared where no child should be. Millions would watch. Few would agree on what they saw.

It began with a wellness check. On the morning of August 13, 2018, Nickole Atkinson, a close friend of Shanann Watts, arrived at the Watts' home in Frederick, Colorado. Shanann had missed a doctor's appointment. Her phone was off. Her car was in the garage. Her purse, phone, and medication were all inside the house. Nickole knew something was wrong. She called the Frederick Police Department.

Officers arrived and entered the home with Chris Watts, who had just returned from "work." The bodycam footage captured everything: the pristine kitchen, the unmade beds, the stripped master bedroom sheets. And then, in an upstairs room, something else. A small child, visible in the background, moving around near a doorway. The light in the room seemed to turn off on its own at the 0:33 mark. Viewers of the Netflix documentary American Murder: The Family Next Door spotted it immediately.

The theory spread like wildfire: the ghost of Bella or Celeste Watts, one of the two young daughters Chris Watts would later confess to murdering, had appeared on camera. The figure was small, child-sized, moving with an eerie stillness. The timing was impossible — the girls were already dead, their bodies hidden in oil tanks at an Anadarko Petroleum site. But skeptics quickly offered another explanation: the child was Nickole Atkinson's own daughter, who had accompanied her mother to the house and was seen playing in several parts of the footage.

The debate continues to this day. Was it a ghost? A living child? A trick of light and shadow? The bodycam footage, released by the Weld County District Attorney's office as part of a massive evidence dump, remains one of the most analyzed pieces of true crime media in history. Frame by frame, millions have searched for answers in the pixels. What they found depends on what they were looking for.

THE QUESTION THAT HAUNTS EVERYONE:
If the figure was Nickole Atkinson's daughter, why did the light turn off by itself? Why did Chris Watts seem to glance toward the room with such unease? And why — despite official explanations — do so many viewers still see something supernatural in those few seconds of footage? The case is closed. The footage is not.

Key Evidence Categories

Bodycam Footage

The Ghost Child Sighting

At approximately the 0:33 mark in the bodycam footage, a small childlike figure appears in an upstairs bedroom doorway. The room light turns off moments before. Chris Watts stands downstairs, visibly nervous, as officers search the home.

Surveillance

Neighbor's Security Camera

Nathan Trinastich's surveillance camera captured Chris Watts loading his truck at 5:18 AM. The footage shows three trips from the house to the truck. Watts would later watch this footage with police, pacing and offering explanations.

Witness

Nickole Atkinson's Testimony

Shanann's best friend was the first to raise the alarm. Her police interview reveals crucial details: the unmade beds, the stripped sheets, the missing purse. She also confirmed her own daughter was present at the house that morning.

Confession

The Confession Tapes

After failing a polygraph test, Chris Watts confessed to murdering Shanann, Bella (4), and Celeste (3). He initially claimed Shanann killed the children and he killed her in rage. He later admitted to all three murders.

Crime Scene

The Oil Site Discovery

Shanann was found buried in a shallow grave near an oil tank battery. Bella and Celeste's bodies were recovered from separate 400-barrel oil tanks. Drone footage shows the desolate location where Watts disposed of his family.

Media

American Murder Documentary

The 2020 Netflix documentary brought the case to a global audience. Director Jenny Popplewell used Shanann's own social media videos, text messages, and police footage to reconstruct the family's final weeks. The ghost child theory exploded after its release.

Case Timeline

Every known movement and event from the morning of the murders to the discovery of the ghost child footage.

August 13, 2018 — 1:48 AM
The Final Hours

Shanann Watts returns home from a business trip in Arizona. Chris Watts murders her, Bella (4), and Celeste (3) in the early morning hours. He later claims he strangled Shanann after she killed the children. He admits this was a lie.

August 13, 2018 — 5:18 AM
The Loading

Neighbor Nathan Trinastich's security camera captures Chris Watts backing his truck into the garage and making three trips carrying items to the truck. Watts loads the bodies of his wife and daughters. He tells police he was loading tools for work.

August 13, 2018 — 12:30 PM
Nickole Raises the Alarm

Nickole Atkinson calls Chris Watts after Shanann misses her doctor's appointment. Watts claims the family is on a play date. Nickole knows this is false — the car and car seats are still at home. She contacts Frederick Police for a wellness check.

August 13, 2018 — Afternoon
The Bodycam Footage

Police enter the Watts home with Chris Watts and Nickole Atkinson. Bodycam footage captures the search: unmade beds, stripped master bedroom sheets, Shanann's purse and phone left behind. In an upstairs room, a small figure appears in the background. The light turns off.

August 13, 2018 — Evening
The Neighbor's Footage

Police take Chris Watts to neighbor Nathan Trinastich's home to view surveillance footage. Watts watches himself loading the truck at 5:18 AM. He paces, offers explanations, and avoids eye contact with the screen. Trinastich later tells police Watts was "trying to cover his tracks."

August 15, 2018
The Polygraph & Confession

After failing a polygraph test administered by CBI Agent Tammy Lee, Chris Watts confesses to his father Ronnie Watts that Shanann killed the children and he killed her. He later admits to murdering all three. He leads police to the oil site where the bodies are recovered.

November 19, 2018
Sentencing

Chris Watts pleads guilty to nine charges including five counts of first-degree murder. He is sentenced to five life sentences without parole. Shanann's family delivers emotional victim impact statements. Watts avoids the death penalty through the plea deal.

September 30, 2020
The Ghost Child Theory Emerges

Netflix releases American Murder: The Family Next Door. Within days, YouTube user Arthur Cant posts a frame-by-frame analysis claiming a ghost child appears in the bodycam footage. The video goes viral. Millions debate: ghost, living child, or optical illusion?

Bodycam Analysis

The 0:33 Sighting

At approximately 33 seconds into the bodycam footage, a small figure appears in an upstairs bedroom doorway. The figure is child-sized, wearing light-colored clothing. It moves briefly before disappearing. The room's light turns off moments before the figure appears.

Digital Forensics

Frame-by-Frame Analysis

YouTube investigator Arthur Cant's viral video breaks down the footage frame by frame. He identifies the figure at 0:33, the light switching off, and Chris Watts' reaction. Critics note the figure matches Nickole Atkinson's daughter's clothing and hair from other footage.

Behavioral

Watts' Body Language

Body language experts analyzing the footage note Watts' nervous pacing, lack of eye contact, and repeated self-explanations. When the neighbor's surveillance plays, he looks at his phone rather than the screen. Experts call this "deception clustering."

Surveillance

The 5:18 AM Timeline

The neighbor's camera establishes a precise timeline. At 5:18 AM, Watts backs into the garage. He makes three trips to the truck. The entire loading process takes approximately 50 minutes. This contradicts his initial statements to police.
