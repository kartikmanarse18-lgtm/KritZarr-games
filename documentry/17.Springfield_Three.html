SPRINGFIELD THREE
Springfield, Missouri, 1992 — A True Crime Investigation

They Were Called The Springfield Three

The glass globe was shattered, but the bulb still burned. It was the only sign that something had gone terribly wrong at 1717 East Delmar Street. Three women— a mother, her daughter, and her daughter\'s best friend— had vanished into the Missouri dawn, leaving behind their purses, their cars, their cigarettes, and a beloved Yorkshire terrier named Cinnamon who would never stop waiting by the door.

It began with a celebration. June 6, 1992, was graduation day at Kickapoo High School in Springfield, Missouri. Stacy McCall, an 18-year-old with long dark blonde hair and light-colored eyes, and her best friend Suzanne "Suzie" Streeter, a 19-year-old with shoulder-length blonde hair and brown eyes, walked across the stage together. They had their whole lives ahead of them— plans to attend Missouri State University, careers to build, dreams to chase.

After the ceremony, the girls changed clothes and set out to celebrate. They attended several graduation parties across Springfield and the surrounding area. Their original plan was to spend the night at a hotel in Branson and visit a water park the next day. But as the night wore on, plans changed. Around 2:00 AM, Suzie and Stacy left their friend Janelle Kirby\'s house in Battlefield, Missouri, and decided to spend the night at Suzie\'s home instead.

Sherrill Levitt, Suzie\'s 47-year-old mother, was already home. A skilled cosmetologist with short bleached-blonde hair, she had been working on renovating the small house at 1717 East Delmar Street. She was a chain smoker, fiercely independent, and deeply protective of her daughter. That night, she was alone except for Cinnamon, the family\'s Yorkshire terrier.

Sometime between 2:15 AM and 7:30 AM, all three women disappeared. When Janelle Kirby arrived the next morning, she found the front door unlocked. The glass globe covering the porch light had been shattered, though the bulb itself remained intact. Inside, the women\'s purses sat on the floor of Suzie\'s bedroom— Sherrill\'s containing nearly $900 in cash from her salon work. Their cars were parked outside. Their cigarettes, keys, and makeup wipes lay undisturbed. The television in Suzie\'s room showed nothing but static snow, as if a movie had ended and no one had been there to turn it off. Cinnamon was frantic, running through the house in distress.

But the three women were gone. No signs of struggle. No blood. No forced entry. Just a broken porch light, a dog that wouldn\'t stop barking, and a mystery that would haunt Springfield for more than three decades.

THE QUESTION THAT HAUNTS EVERYONE:
How do three women vanish from a locked house in the middle of the night without leaving a trace? Who shattered the porch light globe, and why? What was on the answering machine message that was accidentally erased— a message police believed might have contained the only real clue? And why— despite thousands of tips, multiple suspects, and decades of investigation— have Sherrill Levitt, Suzie Streeter, and Stacy McCall never been found? The Springfield Police Department lists the case as active. But the files tell a story of contamination, missed opportunities, and shadows that stretch across the Ozarks.

Key Evidence Categories

Crime Scene

1717 East Delmar Street

The modest home where three women vanished between 2:15 AM and 7:30 AM. The front door was found unlocked, a glass porch light globe shattered but bulb intact. All personal belongings remained inside including purses with cash, cars in the driveway, and the family dog Cinnamon.

Physical Evidence

The Broken Porch Light

The shattered glass globe on the front porch light remains the most perplexing clue. Friend Mike Henson swept up the broken glass before police arrived, potentially destroying evidence. The bulb remained intact and burning— suggesting the globe was broken from outside or during a struggle.

Communication

The Erased Message

Stacy\'s mother Janis McCall discovered a strange message on the answering machine when searching for clues. In her panic, the message was accidentally erased. Police believed it may have contained vital information but it was lost forever— a critical piece of evidence destroyed by grief.

Prime Suspect

Robert Craig Cox

Former Army Ranger and convicted kidnapper who claimed he knew the women were dead and buried in Springfield. He lived in the area at the time of the disappearance and had a history of violence against women. He refused to provide details but hinted at knowledge only the killer would possess.

Obscene Calls

The Prank Caller

When Janelle Kirby answered the phone at the house, an unidentified male made sexual innuendos using obscene language. He called back immediately after she hung up. Suzie had complained about similar prank calls since moving in with her mother. Police do not believe the calls were related to the disappearance.

Investigation

Scene Contamination

Police estimated 10-20 people entered the house before officers arrived. Friends and family cleaned coffee cups, emptied ashtrays, and moved items while searching for clues. Officer Rick Bookout arrived to find a crowded driveway and a compromised scene. The contamination may have destroyed the case.

Case Timeline

Every known movement and event from graduation night to the present investigation.

November 1, 1944
Birth of Sherrill Levitt

Sherrill Elizabeth Levitt is born in Seattle, Washington. She later moves to Springfield, Missouri, where she builds a career as a cosmetologist and raises her daughter Suzanne after a divorce.

March 9, 1973
Birth of Suzie Streeter

Suzanne "Suzie" Elizabeth Streeter is born in Springfield, Missouri, to Sherrill Levitt and Brent Streeter. She grows up to be artistic, outgoing, and popular at Kickapoo High School.

April 23, 1974
Birth of Stacy McCall

Stacy Kathleen McCall is born in Springfield, Missouri, to Janis and Stuart McCall. She grows up in a middle-class family and becomes best friends with Suzie Streeter at Kickapoo High School.

June 6, 1992 — Evening
Graduation Night

Stacy McCall and Suzie Streeter graduate from Kickapoo High School. They attend multiple graduation parties across Springfield and Battlefield, Missouri. Their friend Janelle Kirby joins them for part of the evening.

June 7, 1992 — 2:00 AM
The Last Known Moments

After leaving Janelle Kirby\'s house in Battlefield, Suzie and Stacy decide to spend the night at Suzie\'s home at 1717 East Delmar Street instead of going to a hotel in Branson. Sherrill Levitt is already home. This is the last time anyone sees the three women alive.

June 7, 1992 — 9:00 AM
Discovery

Janelle Kirby and her boyfriend Mike Henson arrive at the house. They find the front door unlocked, the porch light globe shattered, and no sign of the three women. Cinnamon the Yorkshire terrier is agitated. Mike sweeps up the broken glass. Janelle answers two obscene phone calls from an unidentified male.

June 7, 1992 — Afternoon
The Search Begins

Stacy\'s mother Janis McCall arrives at the house and finds the women\'s purses on the floor, clothing neatly folded, and an unfinished can of Coke. She discovers a strange message on the answering machine but accidentally erases it. Police are called but do not arrive for at least a day. Friends and family contaminate the scene.

June 9, 1992
FBI Joins Investigation

The Federal Bureau of Investigation joins the Springfield Police Department and Missouri State Highway Patrol in the search. Massive searches begin in rural and suburban areas around Springfield, including Lake Springfield, Forsyth, Joplin, and Stockton. No trace of the women is found.

1993
Serial Killer Theory

Investigators announce they are exploring the possibility that the three women fell victim to a serial killer. Searches of farms and rural properties yield items of interest but no definitive evidence. The case begins to go cold as leads dry up.

1996
Cox Interview

Convicted criminal Robert Craig Cox, who had been living in Springfield at the time of the disappearance, tells a reporter that he believes the three women are dead and buried in Springfield. He refuses to implicate himself directly but hints at knowledge of their fate. Police reinvestigate him but cannot build a case.

1997
Declared Dead

Sherrill Levitt and Suzie Streeter are legally declared dead. Stacy McCall is also declared dead. Despite no bodies being found, the legal designation reflects the reality that after five years, hope of finding them alive has vanished.

2007
Cox Hospital Parking Lot

A local reporter suggests the trio might be buried beneath the parking lot at Cox Hospital. Ground-penetrating radar identifies three anomalies in the area. However, police determine there is insufficient evidence to justify excavation. The theory remains unproven.

2025-Present
34 Years of Questions

The case remains actively investigated by the Springfield Police Department. New tips continue to come in, but no breakthrough has occurred. The families of Sherrill, Suzie, and Stacy continue to seek answers. The broken porch light still burns in the memories of those who knew them.

The Evidence Room

Every artifact, every photograph, every fragment of glass. The physical remnants of a case that defies explanation.

Physical Evidence

The Shattered Globe

The glass porch light globe at 1717 East Delmar Street was found shattered on the morning of June 7, 1992. Mike Henson, Janelle Kirby's boyfriend, swept up the broken glass before police arrived — a well-meaning act that may have destroyed the only physical evidence left at the scene. The bulb remained intact and burning.

Personal Effects

Three Purses, Three Lives

All three women's purses were found on the floor of Suzie's bedroom. Sherrill's contained nearly $900 in cash from her salon work. Their keys, jewelry, driver's licenses, and cigarettes were all present. Nothing was stolen. The only thing missing was the women themselves.

Witness

Cinnamon: The Only Witness

Sherrill and Suzie's Yorkshire terrier, Cinnamon, was found frantic and agitated when Janelle Kirby arrived. The dog was "barreling through the house" and "yipping and crying" according to Janis McCall. Dogs are notoriously unreliable witnesses — but Cinnamon was the only living being present when three women vanished.

Communication

The Erased Message

Janis McCall discovered a "strange message" on the answering machine while searching for clues. In her panic and grief, the message was accidentally erased. Police believed it "may have contained a clue" and were "very interested" in its contents. It was not connected to the prank calls Janelle received. The message is lost forever.

Vehicles

The Misplaced Cars

Suzie's car was not parked in her usual spot — a detail noted by her coworker Nigel Kenney, who described her as a "creature of habit." This suggests someone else may have been parked in her spot when she and Stacy arrived home around 2:15 AM. Both cars were locked and contained no evidence.

Crime Scene

Suzie's Bedroom

The bed appeared slept-in. The TV showed static snow, as if a movie had ended. Makeup wipes in the bathroom trash indicated Suzie and Stacy had cleaned up before bed. Stacy's clothes were neatly folded. An unfinished can of Coke sat beside cigarettes. The scene suggested they had settled in for the night — then disappeared.

  • Refused to provide details but hinted at knowledge only killer would have

Dustin Recla & Michael Clay
Persons of Interest — Ex-Boyfriend Connection
  • Dustin Recla was Suzie Streeter's ex-boyfriend with motive for revenge
  • Recla and Clay facing vandalism and theft charges from mausoleum incident
  • Suzie was scheduled to testify against them in upcoming trial
  • Both provided alibis and were cooperative with investigators
  • Clay reportedly made some incriminating statements during questioning
  • Eventually pleaded guilty to original charges, sentenced to probation
  • Still considered persons of interest but never charged

The Van Sighting
Witness Report — Unverified Lead
  • Witness reported seeing Suzie driving silver-green Dodge Panel Van at 6:30 AM
  • Suzie appeared "very distressed" taking orders from man in backseat
  • Vehicle was 1964-1970 model, over a mile from crime scene
  • Additional witnesses reported seeing similar van in different Springfield areas
  • One witness wrote down partial license plate but discarded the paper
  • Under hypnosis, witness could only recall three digits of plate
  • Police parked similar van at station seeking tips but no definitive leads emerged

Cox Hospital Parking Garage
Location Theory — GPR Anomalies Found
  • Local reporter suggested bodies buried beneath Cox South Hospital parking lot
  • Ground-penetrating radar scan conducted in 2007 by engineer Rick Norland
  • Three anomalies found "roughly the same size" consistent with grave sites
  • Two anomalies parallel, one perpendicular — unusual pattern
  • Garage construction began September 1993, over a year after disappearances
  • SPD determined insufficient evidence to justify costly excavation
  • Tip reportedly came from psychic or someone claiming dream/vision

  • Police do not believe calls were related to disappearance

Serial Killer Theory
FBI Investigation — February 1993
  • Police announced in February 1993 they were exploring serial killer possibility
  • Three women abducted simultaneously suggests organized, experienced perpetrator
  • Searches of farms and rural properties yielded "items of interest" but no bodies
  • No other similar disappearances in Springfield area around same time
  • Case remains unique in American criminal history for scale of simultaneous abduction

The Unexplained Details

The Answering Machine

Janis McCall heard a "strange message" that alarmed her, but it was erased. Police believed it "may have contained a clue." What was on that tape? A threat? A confession? A wrong number? The only person who heard it was a mother searching for her daughter.

The Sealed Map

An envelope containing a hand-drawn map was found in a Springfield News-Leader rack. It directed investigators to a northwest Springfield apartment complex with the phrase "use Ruse of Gas Man checking for Leak." Weekend search found nothing. Who left it?

The AMW Caller

On December 31, 1992, a man called America's Most Wanted hotline with "prime knowledge of the abductions." The call disconnected when the operator tried to link with Springfield investigators. He never called back. Police publicly appealed for him to contact them.
