YUBA COUNTY FIVE
Plumas National Forest, 1978 — A True Crime Investigation

They Called Them The American Dyatlov Pass

"It was a bad road. He wouldn't drive his car up the bad road. He respected it." Melba Madruga said of her son Jack. Yet on February 24, 1978, five young men from Yuba County drove 70 miles in the wrong direction, up a snow-packed mountain road into the Plumas National Forest, and vanished into one of the most baffling mysteries in American history.

It began with five friends who loved basketball. Jack Madruga, 30, was the quiet, reliable driver who had served in Vietnam and bought his turquoise 1969 Mercury Montego with military money. Bill Sterling, 29, was an avid bowler with a gentle soul. Ted Weiher, 32, was a gentle giant who worked as a janitor and looked after the others. Jackie Huett, 24, was a loving shadow to Ted, dependent on his care. And Gary Mathias, 25, was the outlier—a former Army musician with paranoid schizophrenia, on medication but carrying a troubled past.

They called themselves "the boys." They met through a Yuba County nonprofit basketball program for adults with disabilities. On February 24, 1978, they piled into Jack's Mercury to drive 50 miles north to Chico State University to watch their beloved UC Davis basketball team play. They stopped at Behr's Market for snacks and milk before the 10 PM closing. The clerk remembered them because they delayed her closing routine. That was the last time anyone saw all five together alive.

When morning came and the boys hadn't returned, their families reported them missing. The next day, the Gators—their basketball team—were scheduled to play in a Special Olympics tournament where the winners would get a free week in Disneyland. These men had laid out their uniforms the night before. They were not going to miss that game. Yet they never came home.

Four days later, on February 28, Jack Madruga's Mercury Montego was found abandoned on the Oroville-Quincy Highway in Butte County—70 miles in the wrong direction from home, thousands of feet up in snow-covered mountains. The car had a quarter tank of gas, the doors were unlocked, one window was down, and the snacks had been eaten. The car was not stuck. The keys were gone. And the five men had vanished into a wilderness where temperatures plunged below freezing and snowdrifts reached six feet deep.

THE QUESTION THAT HAUNTS EVERYONE:
Why did five men who knew the route home drive 70 miles in the wrong direction, up a mountain road none of them had any reason to travel? Why did they abandon a working car with gas in the tank? How did four of them end up dead in a Forest Service trailer 19 miles away, with one having starved to death despite ample food nearby? And where is Gary Mathias—the only one never found, the one with schizophrenia, the one whose shoes were left behind in the trailer while another man's shoes went missing? The Yuba County Sheriff's Office still lists the case as active. But the truth, like the snow that buried them, remains frozen in time.

Key Evidence Categories

Crime Scene

The Abandoned Mercury Montego

Jack Madruga's turquoise 1969 Mercury Montego was found on February 28, 1978, on the Oroville-Quincy Highway in Butte County. The car had a quarter tank of gas, was not stuck in snow, doors unlocked, one window down, snacks eaten. The keys were missing. It was 70 miles in the wrong direction from home.

Discovery Site

The Forest Service Trailer

On June 4, 1978, a motorcyclist discovered Ted Weiher's emaciated body inside a USFS trailer 19 miles from the abandoned car. He had survived 8-13 weeks, lost nearly 100 pounds, and was wrapped in eight sheets. The trailer contained a year's worth of canned food and a propane heater—both untouched.

Victim

Ted Weiher — The Survivor

Ted Weiher, 32, was found in the trailer having survived for months. He had a full beard (clean-shaven when he disappeared), lost 80-100 pounds, and suffered from gangrene and frostbite. His boots were missing. Gary Mathias's sneakers were found in the trailer. Ted's wallet and a watch that didn't belong to him were on the nightstand.

Missing Person

Gary Mathias — The One Never Found

Gary Mathias, 25, was the only member never found. A former Army musician with paranoid schizophrenia, he had a history of violence and drug use. His shoes were found in the trailer where Ted died, while Ted's boots were gone. Law enforcement initially suspected Mathias may have been involved in the others' deaths.

Witness

Joseph Schons — The Lone Witness

Joseph Schons told police he was stranded in his car on the same mountain road that night. He saw people with flashlights around a car, heard them call for help twice, but when he called out, they went silent and turned off their lights. He later saw a group of men walking away with a woman. His account remains controversial.

Victims

The Other Three

Jack Madruga, 30, was found near a stream with car keys in his pocket—animals had scattered his remains. Bill Sterling, 29, was reduced to bones scattered across the forest floor. Jackie Huett, 24, was identified when his father picked up his jacket and his son's spine fell out. All were found between the car and the trailer.

Case Timeline

Every known movement and event from the night of the basketball game to the present investigation.

February 24, 1978 — Evening
The Basketball Game

The five men attend the UC Davis vs. Chico State basketball game at California State University, Chico. They are seen sitting away from the crowd, distinctive in their behavior. The editor of the Chico newspaper later recalls seeing them. After the game, they stop at Behr's Market for snacks, milk, and sodas before the 10 PM closing.

February 24-25, 1978 — Night
The Disappearance

Instead of driving 50 miles south on Highway 70 back to Yuba City, the group drives 70 miles east into the Sierra Nevada mountains on the Oroville-Quincy Highway. Joseph Schons, stranded in his car on the same road, sees flashlights and hears voices. When he calls for help, the lights go out and voices fall silent. He later sees figures walking away.

February 25, 1978 — Morning
The Search Begins

The men's families report them missing when they fail to return home. The Gators basketball team is scheduled to play in a Special Olympics tournament that morning—the winners would receive a free week at Disneyland. The men had laid out their uniforms the night before. They never arrive. Search efforts begin immediately but focus on the wrong areas.

February 28, 1978
The Car Is Found

Jack Madruga's 1969 Mercury Montego is discovered on the Oroville-Quincy Highway in Butte County, 70 miles from Chico and far from the route home. The car is not stuck, has a quarter tank of gas, doors are unlocked, one window is down, and snacks are eaten. The keys and the men are missing. Snow is 4-6 feet deep. Temperatures are below freezing.

June 4, 1978
Ted Weiher Discovered

A motorcyclist off-roading near the Daniel Zink campground discovers Ted Weiher's body in a USFS trailer. He is on a bed, wrapped in eight sheets, hands folded on his chest. He has a full beard, has lost 80-100 pounds, and shows signs of gangrene and frostbite. A propane heater and a year's worth of canned food are untouched nearby. Gary Mathias's shoes are in the trailer; Ted's boots are gone.

June 6-8, 1978
The Others Are Found

Search teams find the remains of Jack Madruga and Bill Sterling on opposite sides of the mountain road, about eight miles from the car and closer to the trailer. Madruga's car keys are still in his pocket. Sterling is reduced to scattered bones. On June 8, Jackie Huett's father finds his son's jacket; when he picks it up, Jackie's spine falls out. His skull is found 50 feet away.

June 19, 1978
Search Called Off

After two weeks of searching with no sign of Gary Mathias, investigators call off the search. Mathias's family is left without closure. The case officially remains open but goes cold. Theories range from Mathias wandering deeper into the wilderness to foul play involving an unknown party.

2003-Present
The Investigation Continues

Yuba County Sheriff's Office cold case investigators, including Brian Bernardis, continue to revisit the case. Author Tony Wright spends four years researching and publishes "Things Aren't Right: The Disappearance of the Yuba County Five." The case gains renewed attention as the "American Dyatlov Pass," drawing comparisons to the 1959 Russian incident. Gary Mathias remains missing.

The Evidence

Forensic findings, crime scene analysis, and the physical artifacts that define the Yuba County Five case.

Forensic

The Working Car

Madruga's Mercury Montego was found in working condition with a quarter tank of gas. The car was not stuck in snow and could have been pushed free. The doors were unlocked, one window was down, and snacks had been eaten. Yet the keys and the men were gone. Why would five men abandon a working car in freezing temperatures without proper clothing?

Forensic

The Untouched Supplies

The USFS trailer contained a year's worth of canned food in an outside locker, a propane heater, and burnable wood and paper. Only 31 cans were opened and eaten. The propane tank was untouched. A candle had been lit but no fire was started. The food was opened with an Army P-38 can opener—only Mathias and Madruga had military experience with this tool.

Crime Scene

Ted Weiher's Condition

Weiher survived 8-13 weeks based on beard growth. He lost 80-100 pounds from his 200-pound frame. He had gangrene in his legs, five toes lost to frostbite, and his pant legs were rolled up. He was wrapped in eight sheets with hands folded on his chest. A watch that didn't belong to him and his wallet were on the nightstand. His boots were missing.

Evidence

The Shoe Swap

Gary Mathias's sneakers were found inside the trailer where Ted Weiher died. Ted Weiher's own leather boots were missing. Investigators believe Mathias may have swapped shoes because Ted's boots were better suited for snow. This suggests Mathias was in the trailer and left after Ted died—or that Ted wore Mathias's shoes when he left.

Theories & Suspects

Nearly five decades of investigation have produced multiple theories. These are the most compelling explanations for what happened.

⚖
Official: Death by Exposure
Yuba County Sheriff Status: Active Cold Case
  • Men got lost on mountain road during snowstorm
  • Abandoned car and tried to find shelter on foot
  • Four died of hypothermia/exposure in wilderness
  • Ted Weiher found shelter but succumbed to starvation
  • Gary Mathias likely perished in the wilderness
  • No evidence of foul play: no bullet holes, stab wounds, or weapons

Gary Mathias Theory
Probability: HIGH (per initial investigators)
  • Mathias was only member with paranoid schizophrenia
  • History of violence; charged with assault twice
  • Only member never found; body never recovered
  • His shoes found in trailer; Ted's boots missing
  • May have had psychotic episode leading group astray
  • Family noted he sometimes stopped taking medication
  • However: No signs of disturbance at basketball game

Foul Play / Abduction Theory
Probability: MODERATE (per families)
  • Joseph Schons saw flashlights and heard voices—then silence
  • Group had no reason to drive 70 miles in wrong direction
  • Madruga was protective of his car; wouldn't drive bad roads
  • Men were dressed for valley weather, not mountain snow
  • Ted Weiher was wrapped in sheets as if cared for
  • Rumors of fight at convenience store; town bully confession
  • "Somebody made them" — Perry Weiher
🔵
Wrong Turn / Forbestown Theory
Probability: MODERATE
  • Mathias had friends in Forbestown near the mountain road
  • Possible they took wrong turn near Oroville
  • May have followed snowcat tracks thinking shelter was near
  • Group had cognitive disabilities affecting judgment
  • Storm conditions may have disoriented Madruga
  • However: Madruga knew the area and was reliable driver
🔵
The "Bizarre as Hell" Theory
Probability: UNKNOWN
  • Jack Beecham, Yuba County Undersheriff: "Bizarre as hell"
  • Ted survived months but didn't use available food/heat
  • Army P-38 can opener used—only Mathias/Madruga knew how
  • Watch on nightstand didn't belong to any victim
  • Weiher's pant legs rolled up revealing gangrene
  • Five toes lost to frostbite; body in state of decomposition
  • Case defies logical explanation
🔴
The Dyatlov Connection
Probability: SPECULATIVE
  • Both cases involve groups dying in remote winter wilderness
  • Inexplicable decisions to leave shelter/safety
  • Bodies found in bizarre circumstances
  • No satisfactory explanation for behavior
  • Yuba County Five: 1978 California; Dyatlov: 1959 Russia
  • Both cases remain officially unsolved
