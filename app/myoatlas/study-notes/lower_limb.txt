## HIP & GLUTEAL
gluteus maximus | Posterior ilium behind the posterior gluteal line, sacrum, coccyx, sacrotuberous ligament | Iliotibial tract (most) and gluteal tuberosity of the femur | Inferior gluteal n. (L5–S2) | Extends and laterally rotates the thigh; extends the trunk from a flexed position (stairs, running, rising from a chair) | Largest muscle in the body. Intramuscular injections avoid it: use the upper outer quadrant or ventrogluteal site.
gluteus medius | External ilium between the anterior and posterior gluteal lines | Lateral surface of the greater trochanter | Superior gluteal n. (L4–S1) | Abducts and medially rotates the thigh; holds the pelvis level in single-leg stance | Trendelenburg sign: pelvis drops on the opposite side when standing on the affected leg.
gluteus minimus | External ilium between the anterior and inferior gluteal lines | Anterior surface of the greater trochanter | Superior gluteal n. (L4–S1) | Abducts and medially rotates the thigh; stabilises the pelvis
tensor fasciae latae | Anterior superior iliac spine and anterior iliac crest | Iliotibial tract, to the lateral tibial condyle (Gerdy tubercle) | Superior gluteal n. (L4–L5) | Abducts, flexes and medially rotates the thigh; tightens the iliotibial tract | Iliotibial band syndrome in runners.
piriformis | Anterior surface of the sacrum (S2–S4) and sacrotuberous ligament | Superior border of the greater trochanter | Nerve to piriformis (S1–S2) | Laterally rotates the extended thigh; abducts the flexed thigh | Landmark of the gluteal region; the sciatic nerve usually exits below it (piriformis syndrome).
obturator internus | Inner surface of the obturator membrane and surrounding bone | Medial surface of the greater trochanter | Nerve to obturator internus (L5–S1) | Laterally rotates the extended thigh; abducts the flexed thigh | Tendon leaves the pelvis through the lesser sciatic foramen.
superior gemellus | Ischial spine | Medial surface of the greater trochanter (with obturator internus tendon) | Nerve to obturator internus (L5–S1) | Laterally rotates the thigh
inferior gemellus | Ischial tuberosity | Medial surface of the greater trochanter (with obturator internus tendon) | Nerve to quadratus femoris (L5–S1) | Laterally rotates the thigh
quadratus femoris | Lateral border of the ischial tuberosity | Quadrate tubercle on the intertrochanteric crest | Nerve to quadratus femoris (L5–S1) | Laterally rotates and stabilises the hip
iliacus | Iliac fossa | Lesser trochanter (via the iliopsoas tendon) | Femoral n. (L2–L3) | Flexes the hip; stabilises the hip joint and trunk | Together with psoas major forms iliopsoas, the strongest hip flexor.
psoas major | Transverse processes of the lumbar vertebrae; sides of T12–L5 bodies and intervertebral discs | Lesser trochanter of the femur | Ventral rami of L1–L3 | Flexes the thigh; flexes the lumbar spine | Psoas sign for appendicitis or psoas abscess: pain on hip extension.

## THIGH — ANTERIOR (femoral nerve)
rectus femoris | Anterior inferior iliac spine and superior rim of the acetabulum | Base of the patella via the quadriceps tendon, then the tibial tuberosity | Femoral n. (L2–L4) | Extends the knee; flexes the hip | The only quadriceps head that crosses the hip.
vastus lateralis | Greater trochanter and lateral lip of the linea aspera | Base and lateral border of the patella, then the tibial tuberosity | Femoral n. (L2–L4) | Extends the knee | Largest quadriceps head; the vastus lateralis is a common site for IM injections in infants.
vastus medialis | Intertrochanteric line and medial lip of the linea aspera | Base and medial border of the patella, then the tibial tuberosity | Femoral n. (L2–L4) | Extends the knee; stabilises the patella (oblique fibres) | Weakness predisposes to patellar maltracking.
vastus intermedius | Anterior and lateral surfaces of the femoral shaft | Base of the patella via the quadriceps tendon | Femoral n. (L2–L4) | Extends the knee | Lies deep to rectus femoris.
sartorius | Anterior superior iliac spine | Superomedial surface of the tibia (pes anserinus) | Femoral n. (L2–L3) | Flexes, abducts and laterally rotates the hip; flexes the knee | "Tailor's muscle", the longest muscle in the body. Forms the lateral border of the femoral triangle.

## THIGH — MEDIAL (obturator nerve)
pectineus | Pecten pubis (superior pubic ramus) | Pectineal line of the femur | Femoral n. (L2–L3); sometimes obturator | Flexes and adducts the thigh | Floor of the femoral triangle.
adductor longus | Body of the pubis, below the pubic crest | Middle third of the linea aspera | Obturator n. (anterior division, L2–L4) | Adducts the thigh | Forms the medial border of the femoral triangle; commonly strained (groin strain).
adductor brevis | Body and inferior ramus of the pubis | Pectineal line and upper linea aspera | Obturator n. (L2–L4) | Adducts the thigh
adductor magnus | Adductor part: inferior pubic ramus and ischial ramus. Hamstring part: ischial tuberosity | Gluteal tuberosity, linea aspera and adductor tubercle | Adductor part: obturator n.; hamstring part: tibial division of sciatic n. (L2–L4) | Adducts the thigh; hamstring part extends the hip | "Part adductor, part hamstring". Adductor hiatus transmits the femoral vessels.
adductor minimus | Inferior ramus of the pubis | Upper linea aspera | Obturator n. (L2–L4) | Adducts and flexes the thigh | Upper fibres of adductor magnus.
gracilis | Body and inferior ramus of the pubis | Superomedial surface of the tibia (pes anserinus) | Obturator n. (L2–L3) | Adducts the thigh; flexes the knee | Only adductor that crosses the knee. Used for muscle flaps and grafts.
obturator externus | External surface of the obturator membrane and bony margin | Trochanteric fossa of the femur | Obturator n. (posterior division, L3–L4) | Laterally rotates the thigh; stabilises the hip

## THIGH — POSTERIOR (hamstrings; sciatic nerve)
biceps femoris | Long head: ischial tuberosity. Short head: linea aspera and lateral supracondylar line | Head of the fibula | Long head: tibial division of sciatic n. (S1–S3). Short head: common fibular division (L5–S2) | Flexes the knee; long head extends the hip; laterally rotates the flexed knee | Common fibular nerve runs behind its tendon (can be injured at the fibular neck).
part:long head of biceps femoris | Ischial tuberosity | Head of the fibula | Tibial part of sciatic n.; extends the hip
part:short head of biceps femoris | Lateral lip of the linea aspera | Head of the fibula | Common fibular part of sciatic n.; the only hamstring that does not cross the hip
semitendinosus | Ischial tuberosity | Superomedial surface of the tibia (pes anserinus) | Tibial division of sciatic n. (L5–S2) | Extends the hip; flexes the knee; medially rotates the flexed knee | Long, cord-like tendon in the lower half.
semimembranosus | Ischial tuberosity | Posterior surface of the medial tibial condyle (oblique popliteal ligament) | Tibial division of sciatic n. (L5–S2) | Extends the hip; flexes the knee; medially rotates the flexed knee | Semimembranosus bursa may form a Baker cyst.

## LEG
tibialis anterior | Lateral tibial condyle, upper two thirds of the lateral tibial shaft, interosseous membrane | Medial cuneiform and base of the 1st metatarsal | Deep fibular n. (L4–L5) | Dorsiflexes and inverts the foot | Weakness causes foot drop (steppage gait). Shin splints.
extensor hallucis longus | Middle anterior fibula and interosseous membrane | Base of the distal phalanx of the great toe | Deep fibular n. (L5–S1) | Extends the great toe; dorsiflexes the foot
extensor digitorum longus | Lateral tibial condyle, anterior fibula, interosseous membrane | Middle and distal phalanges of toes 2–5 | Deep fibular n. (L5–S1) | Extends toes 2–5; dorsiflexes the foot
fibularis tertius | Distal anterior fibula | Base of the 5th metatarsal | Deep fibular n. (L5–S1) | Dorsiflexes and everts the foot | Absent in some individuals.
fibularis longus | Head and upper two thirds of the lateral fibula | Medial cuneiform and base of the 1st metatarsal | Superficial fibular n. (L5–S2) | Everts the foot; weakly plantarflexes; supports the arches | Tendon runs under the cuboid in a groove, like a stirrup under the foot.
fibularis brevis | Distal two thirds of the lateral fibula | Tuberosity at the base of the 5th metatarsal | Superficial fibular n. (L5–S2) | Everts the foot; weakly plantarflexes | Avulsion fracture of the 5th metatarsal base in ankle sprains.
gastrocnemius | Medial head: medial femoral condyle. Lateral head: lateral femoral condyle | Posterior surface of the calcaneus via the calcaneal (Achilles) tendon | Tibial n. (S1–S2) | Plantarflexes the foot; flexes the knee | Crosses two joints. Ankle jerk tests S1–S2.
part:medial head of gastrocnemius | Medial femoral condyle (popliteal surface) | Calcaneus via the Achilles tendon | Larger and extends lower than the lateral head
part:lateral head of gastrocnemius | Lateral femoral condyle | Calcaneus via the Achilles tendon | Lateral head
soleus | Posterior fibular head and upper shaft, soleal line and medial border of the tibia | Calcaneus via the Achilles tendon | Tibial n. (S1–S2) | Plantarflexes the foot; essential for standing posture | Acts as the "peripheral heart": the calf pump returns venous blood; site of DVT.
plantaris | Lateral supracondylar line of the femur | Calcaneus, medial to the Achilles tendon | Tibial n. (S1–S2) | Weakly plantarflexes the foot and flexes the knee | Vestigial; its long thin tendon is used for grafts.
popliteus | Lateral femoral condyle and lateral meniscus | Posterior surface of the tibia above the soleal line | Tibial n. (L4–S1) | "Unlocks" the extended knee by laterally rotating the femur; flexes the knee | Forms part of the floor of the popliteal fossa.
tibialis posterior | Interosseous membrane and posterior surfaces of tibia and fibula | Navicular tuberosity, cuneiforms, cuboid and bases of metatarsals 2–4 | Tibial n. (L4–L5) | Inverts the foot; plantarflexes; supports the medial longitudinal arch | Dysfunction causes adult-acquired flatfoot. Tom, Dick And Harry (behind the medial malleolus): Tibialis posterior, flexor Digitorum longus, Artery/vein/nerve, flexor Hallucis longus.
flexor digitorum longus | Posterior surface of the tibia (medial) | Bases of the distal phalanges of toes 2–5 | Tibial n. (S2–S3) | Flexes toes 2–5; plantarflexes the foot
flexor hallucis longus | Posterior surface of the fibula (distal two thirds) and interosseous membrane | Base of the distal phalanx of the great toe | Tibial n. (S2–S3) | Flexes the great toe; push-off in walking | Tendon passes under the sustentaculum tali.

## FOOT
extensor digitorum brevis | Superolateral calcaneus and inferior extensor retinaculum | Extensor expansions of toes 2–4 (joining the long tendons) | Deep fibular n. (L5–S1) | Extends toes 2–4 | The only intrinsic muscle on the dorsum of the foot (with extensor hallucis brevis); a soft swelling in front of the lateral malleolus may be mistaken for a mass.
extensor hallucis brevis | Superolateral calcaneus | Base of the proximal phalanx of the great toe | Deep fibular n. (L5–S1) | Extends the great toe at the MTP joint
abductor hallucis | Medial calcaneal tuberosity, flexor retinaculum, plantar aponeurosis | Medial base of the proximal phalanx of the great toe | Medial plantar n. (S2–S3) | Abducts and flexes the great toe; supports the medial arch | Plantar layer 1 (superficial).
flexor digitorum brevis | Medial calcaneal tuberosity and plantar aponeurosis | Sides of the middle phalanges of toes 2–5 | Medial plantar n. (S2–S3) | Flexes the proximal IP joints of toes 2–5; supports the longitudinal arch | Plantar layer 1; compare with flexor digitorum superficialis in the hand.
abductor digiti minimi of foot | Medial and lateral calcaneal tubercles | Lateral base of the proximal phalanx of the 5th toe | Lateral plantar n. (S2–S3) | Abducts and flexes the little toe | Plantar layer 1 (superficial).
quadratus plantae | Medial and lateral surfaces of the calcaneus | Lateral border of the flexor digitorum longus tendon | Lateral plantar n. (S2–S3) | Straightens the pull of flexor digitorum longus; assists flexion of toes 2–5 | Also called flexor accessorius. Plantar layer 2.
lumbrical of foot | Tendons of flexor digitorum longus | Medial side of the extensor expansions of toes 2–5 | 1st: medial plantar n.; 2nd–4th: lateral plantar n. (S2–S3) | Flex the MTP joints and extend the IP joints | Plantar layer 2.
flexor hallucis brevis | Cuboid and lateral cuneiform | Both sides of the base of the proximal phalanx of the great toe (with sesamoids) | Medial plantar n. (S2–S3) | Flexes the great toe at the MTP joint | Plantar layer 3. Sesamoid bones lie in its tendons.
part:medial head of flexor hallucis brevis | Medial cuneiform and tibialis posterior tendon | Medial base of proximal phalanx of the great toe | Medial head
part:lateral head of flexor hallucis brevis | Cuboid and lateral cuneiform | Lateral base of proximal phalanx of the great toe | Lateral head
adductor hallucis | Oblique head: bases of metatarsals 2–4 and long plantar ligament. Transverse head: plantar ligaments of MTP joints 3–5 | Lateral base of the proximal phalanx of the great toe | Lateral plantar n., deep branch (S2–S3) | Adducts the great toe; maintains the transverse arch | Plantar layer 3.
part:oblique head of adductor hallucis | Bases of metatarsals 2–4, long plantar ligament | Lateral base of proximal phalanx of the great toe | Oblique head
part:transverse head of adductor hallucis | Plantar ligaments of the MTP joints of toes 3–5 | Lateral base of proximal phalanx of the great toe | Transverse head
flexor digiti minimi of foot | Base of the 5th metatarsal | Base of the proximal phalanx of the 5th toe | Lateral plantar n. (S2–S3) | Flexes the little toe at the MTP joint | Plantar layer 3.
opponens digiti minimi of foot | Long plantar ligament and sheath of fibularis longus | Lateral side of the 5th metatarsal | Lateral plantar n. (S2–S3) | Draws the 5th metatarsal plantarward | Often a deep slip of flexor digiti minimi brevis.
dorsal interossei of foot | Adjacent sides of two metatarsals | Bases of proximal phalanges of toes 2–4 (both sides of the 2nd toe) | Lateral plantar n. (S2–S3) | Abduct toes 2–4 away from the 2nd toe (DAB); flex the MTP joints | Plantar layer 4 (deepest); four muscles.
plantar interossei | Medial sides of metatarsals 3–5 | Medial base of the proximal phalanges of toes 3–5 | Lateral plantar n. (S2–S3) | Adduct toes 3–5 toward the 2nd toe (PAD) | Plantar layer 4 (deepest); three muscles.
